** The scenario **
A university advising office tracks student outreach in spreadsheets and email threads. Advisors can't easily see 
which students haven't been contacted recently, or what was discussed last time. They've asked for a simple internal
tool.

You'll receive a small dataset of 50 fictional students (attached zip file) and the history of past advising contacts.
Build a working prototype that advisors could realistically start using.

** What to build **
* Required *

    A front end (React preferred) where an advisor can list, search, and filter students, open a student's detail, and 
    log a new contact note
    
    A simple back-end API that serves the data and saves new notes (any language, framework, and storage is fine)
    
    One "smart" feature of your choice that would help an advisor decide who to reach out to or what to say.
    Examples: a suggested outreach list, or a summary of a student's past notes. Pick whatever you think matters most.

* Not required *

    Authentication, deployment, or a polished design
    Salesforce knowledge. We'll ask how you'd approach connecting this to an enterprise CRM, but you don't 
    need to build or know it.

* Your data *
You'll find two files plus a README in the folder we send you:

    students.csv — one row per student (name, major, class year, credits, GPA, advisor, last contact date, risk flag)
    contact_notes.csv — past advising contacts linked to students by student_id
    README.txt — column descriptions

All names, emails, and notes are entirely fictional. Treat "today" as October 5, 2026. As with real data, this set is
not perfect. Use your judgment about how your tool should handle what you find, and tell us what you noticed and 
what you decided.

** Ground rules **

    Use any tools you like, including AI. We want to hear how you used them: what they helped with, and where you had
    to step in. You are responsible for everything you submit. Scope is part of the exercise. You won't finish
    everything you can imagine in three hours, and we don't expect you to. Cut what you need to, and be ready to tell
    us what you cut and why. Working beats pretty. We'd rather see a smaller thing that runs than a larger thing
    that doesn't. It's fine to run locally. A hosted version is optional.

** What to send us **
By the deadline above, upload to the Box folder:

    Your code (a zip file or a repository link)
    A short README (one page or less) covering:
        How to run it
        Who you built it for and what you assumed about them
        What you cut or left for later
        How you used AI tools, if you did

Your presentation (15 minutes)
You don't need slides. A live demo of the working prototype is ideal. Please cover:

    Who did you build this for, and what did you assume about them?
    What did you cut, and why?
    Where did AI help, and where did you have to step in?
    What would you ask the advisors before building a second version?
    How would you connect this to an enterprise CRM, and what could go wrong?

We'll then spend about 10 minutes asking questions about your choices.
What we're looking for
We care about how you think and work as much as the code itself:

    Does it work end to end?
    Are your scoping decisions sensible and clearly explained?
    Did you think about the people who would use it?
    Can you explain your work clearly to both technical and non-technical colleagues?
    Can you describe your use of AI tools honestly and specifically?

A few practical notes

    We will not use your work. This exercise is for evaluation only. Nothing you build will be used in any
    University system, and the code stays yours. Your time matters. We know you may be working full-time. If you need
    an accommodation or a different format, please contact Meredith (maronson@arizona.edu) and we'll work something out.
    If something is unclear, make assumptions and move forward, but share those in your presentation.  If something is 
    blatantly wrong, please reach out to Meredith (maronson@arizona.edu)

Thank you, and good luck. We're looking forward to seeing what you build.
